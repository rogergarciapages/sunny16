About the Sunny 16 Rule Field Guide

A Free Optical Pocket Guide & Exposure Calculator for 35mm Analog Film Photographers

The Mission: A Classic Field Companion in the Digital Era

Sunny 16 Rule (https://sunny16rule.com) was created to give analog photographers an authentic, fast, reliable, battery-free exposure and depth-of-field field guide inspired by classic 1960s and 1970s Kodak Master Photoguides and Ilford exposure calculators. As mechanical cameras continue to outlive their original light meters and mercury batteries, sunny16rule.com provides the knowledge needed to shoot confidently without relying on electronics or secondary smartphone light meters.

Who It Is For: Mechanical SLRs, Rangefinders & Failed Meters

Built specifically for photographers shooting classic 35mm and medium-format mechanical cameras—such as the Nikon F, FM, and FM2, Leica M2, M3, and M4, Canon F-1 and FTb, Pentax Spotmatic and K1000, Olympus OM-1, Minolta SR-T 101, Yashica Mat-124G, or vintage rangefinders—where built-in selenium or CdS light meters have failed, drifted out of factory calibration, or require banned 1.35V mercury batteries. Whether you are walking city streets with a meterless Leica or shooting landscapes with a folding medium-format camera, Sunny 16 gives you direct, repeatable exposure control.

How Our Optical & Exposure Math Works

The Sunny 16 Rule Calculator is not a static cheat sheet. Our optical calculation engine combines:

  • EV Lighting Values: Scientifically derived Exposure Values (EV 7 to EV 16 at ISO 100) mapping 10 real-world atmospheric conditions to incident light levels.
  • ASA/ISO Reciprocal Shutter Speeds: Exact 1/ISO mathematical calibration with physical hardware-limit checks matched to each camera's physical shutter speed dial (1s to 1/1000s, 1/4000s, or 1/8000s).
  • Cumulative Multi-Ring Filter Factors: True logarithmic stop compensation (+0 to +6 EV) for screw-in contrast filters (Yellow #8, Orange #16, Red #25, Green #11), Circular Polarizers (CPL), 85B warming filters, and ND filters.
  • Circle of Confusion & Depth of Field: Rigorous optical formulas using a 0.029mm circle of confusion for 35mm full-frame (24×36mm) and 0.020mm for 35mm half-frame (18×24mm), calculating near limit, far limit, hyperfocal distance (H), and background blur circle ratios.

Designed in the Spirit of 1968 Technical Manuals

Every layout, typography choice, condensed heading, and paper background was crafted to honor mid-century industrial camera manuals—clean, functional, high-contrast in direct daylight, and completely free of digital clutter.
